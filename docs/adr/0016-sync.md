# 0016 — Sync between devices

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-03 (#36)
- Amends [ADR 0002](0002-local-first-storage.md): no leagues, so no server XP and no plausibility caps

## Context

M8 was planned as sync and weekly leagues. The maintainer dropped leagues on 2026-11-03 (#295): they would have made a DPIA mandatory by ranking learners from 15, and they need more active members than the service has. What remains is the goal of sync: a learner finds their progress on another device. Exit criterion: two devices converge.

Learning keeps working offline and without an account. Sync is opt-in and needs an account.

## Findings (2026-11-03)

- **Merge.** `importBackup` already merges by ids and is idempotent: the union of review logs, cards replayed, decks of the same name merged. It knows no deletions, so a word taken out on one device would come back from the other.
- **Cards.** A card's subject and id never change and its state is derived from the log. Reviews stay in the log when a card is deleted, which is how progress comes back when a word is filed again.
- **Recording changes.** A Dexie hook can record every write in one place. Tested with Dexie 4.4.7 and fake-indexeddb: the record shares the write's transaction and rolls back with it, and works for `bulkPut` and `bulkDelete`. A hook writing to a table outside the transaction's scope only rejects its own write, and the data write still commits, so **the change would be lost in silence**. A hook that throws when the table is out of scope refuses the write instead. Six modules write to the synced tables, some without an explicit transaction, so they all need that scope.
- **Limits.** [D1 on the free plan](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/): 500 MB per database, 2 MB per row, and [100,000 rows written and 5 million read a day](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/). Past a daily limit every query fails until 00:00 UTC, so sign-in and the library fail too. A [result's `meta.size_after`](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/worker-api/return-object/) gives the database size. Functions have 10 ms of CPU per request (ADR 0015).
- **Size.** Measured on a synthetic log of 18,250 reviews (50 a day for a year, 2,000 cards) sent in batches of 50: 199 bytes per review in JSON, 41 once gzipped, so 0.75 MB a year per learner. One row per review would take about 3.6 MB a year before indexes, about a hundred learners for a year. Real logs may differ.
- **R2**, rejected: [10 GB free](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/), but usage beyond it is billed, and the [EU jurisdiction](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/data-location/) is documented for Workers bindings, not for Pages. Chunks can move there later without changing their format.
- **Law.** Hosting is "the storage of information provided by, and at the request of, a recipient of the service" ([DSA art. 3(g)(iii)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065)), with no public element; sync is not an online platform (art. 3(i)), as nothing is disseminated. [LCEN art. 6](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000801164), read in its version of 2026-08-26, takes that definition (I.2). Its V.A requires hosts to keep what identifies "quiconque a contribué à la création du contenu ou de l'un des contenus des services", on pain of one year in prison and a 250,000 euro fine (V.B). [Décret 2021-1362](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000044228912) speaks of a "contenu mis en ligne" in its title, but its arts 5 to 7 cover every creation, modification and deletion. Whether private storage falls under it is not settled by the text. This is not legal advice.
- **DPIA.** Without leagues, criterion 1 of WP248 (scoring) no longer applies. One criterion remains (7, accounts from 15), so a DPIA should no longer be mandatory ([screening](../compliance/dpia-screening.md)). To check again in #296.

## Decision

### What is synced

Reviews, cards, personal decks and their words, notes, and « Je connais » marks. The marks are added to the [data map](../compliance/data-map.md), as a word's coverage would otherwise differ between devices. Journal, challenges, texts, settings, audio, voice and the AI key stay on the device.

### On the device

- **`syncEntities` table.** One row per synced entity: its table and key, the time of its last change, whether it is deleted, whether it is waiting to be sent. A review's row is removed once sent, as a review never changes.
- **Hooks** on the synced tables fill it, in the write's transaction. A write outside a transaction that includes `syncEntities` throws. Cards record only their creation and deletion, as their state is derived. When sync is off, rows are still recorded, so a tab open during activation loses nothing, and they are cleared when the app starts.
- **Sending.** Waiting rows are read with their current values, gzipped in the browser with `CompressionStream` and posted as one chunk of at most 256 KiB, as many as needed. A browser without it sees sync as unavailable.
- **Receiving.** Each chunk is gunzipped and validated with zod, then applied in one transaction with the hooks silenced, and the cursor moves in that transaction. Reviews merge by id. Other entities keep the latest change, a deletion included, compared by time; on a tie the deletion wins, then the larger serialized value, so devices cannot end up different. A deck of the same name merges as in `importBackup`, and a word whose deck was deleted goes with it. Cards are then replayed with the code `importBackup` uses.
- **Chunk format.** `{ format, version, reviews, entities }`. A newer version than the app knows stops the sync and asks for an update.
- **When.** At app start, at the end of a session and when the network comes back, at most every 5 minutes, and on « Sync now ». No background task or notification.

### On the server

- **`sync_chunk` table:** an autoincrement id used as the cursor, `user_id` with a cascading foreign key, the gzip bytes, their size, the date.
- **`/api/sync`** behind the session, as `/api/account`:
  - `POST /chunks`: stores the body, opaque except for its size and gzip header. Answers the chunk's id.
  - `GET /chunks/next?after=<id>`: the next chunk as bytes with its id in a header, or 204. One per request, so nothing is decoded and the CPU limit holds.
  - `DELETE /chunks`: deletes the learner's chunks.
  - Each answer carries the usage, for the screen, and the id of the account's oldest chunk, so a device can see the store was deleted.
- **Limits**, refused with a code the screen explains: 256 KiB per chunk, 10 MiB per account (about 13 years at 50 reviews a day, with room for a large Anki history), 120 chunks an hour per account in the existing `rate_limit` table, and no new chunk once `meta.size_after` exceeds 400 MB. The 500 MB it protects is a provider fact: dated with its source in `compliance-facts.ts`, with a test that fails at `reviewBy`.
- No compaction: at this rate the quota is years away, and full is a visible refusal.
- The server stores no device id: reading back one's own chunk changes nothing.

### Décret 2021-1362: the strict reading

The maintainer chose the strict reading on 2026-11-03: the penalty is criminal and personal, and removing records later is possible where adding them after the fact is not.

- Each chunk stored and each deletion writes a `hosting_record` in the batch of the write: IP address, protocol, time, with `submission_id` holding `sync:<chunk id>` or `sync:all`. Reading writes nothing. The existing job purges them after 1 year.
- `accountDeletion` already seals the identity of an account with a hosting record, so an account that synced keeps its email for 5 years and its pseudonym for 1 year after deletion.
- The activation screen, the privacy notice and the deletion screen say so. The synced data itself is deleted, whichever reading applies.

If legal advice ever says private storage is out of scope, this part is removed and the records purged.

### Turning it on and off

- **Activation** is an explicit choice after a plain explanation: what goes to the server, what is kept for how long, that device and account data will merge. It is never prompted after a session. It marks every entity as waiting, so the first upload holds everything.
- **Turning off** deletes the data from the server, for every device of the account, and keeps the device's.
- **Turned off elsewhere:** a device that remembers another oldest chunk stops and says so. Turning it on again uploads everything, so the store is never left partial.
- **Signing out** stops sync and keeps the device's data.
- **Another account on the same device:** sync is off until turned on again, with the merge warning. The local state remembers which account it belongs to.

### Failures

Nothing is ever skipped or defaulted in silence.

| Case | What happens |
|---|---|
| Unreadable or invalid chunk, or a newer format | Nothing applied, cursor unmoved, error shown, sync stopped there |
| Offline, or a server error | Changes wait; the error stays visible until a sync succeeds |
| Quota or 400 MB reached | Sending refused and explained; local data untouched; receiving still works |
| Daily D1 limit reached | Same as a server error |

### Privacy

- The account export includes the chunks (art. 15, 20), decoded on the device so the server decodes nothing.
- Account deletion removes them by cascade, and a Time Travel restore is covered by the erasure log (ADR 0015).
- Chunks are not end-to-end encrypted: that needs a passphrase a learner can lose, in an app with no passwords. They are opaque to the routes, not to the maintainer, who could decode them and see review times. The notice and the DPIA say so, and a direct read follows the rules of [accounts security](../compliance/accounts-security.md).
- #296 is rescoped to sync: the DPIA, screening, data map, records, retention, notice and terms are updated, and leagues removed from them, before sync reaches production. Sync ships with the next promotion after that, so no flag is needed.

### Interface and tests

- A section in Profile › Account: explanation, switch, last sync, usage, « Sync now », « Turn off and delete from the server ». Mockups are approved before UI code (ADR 0008), with strings in French and English.
- Server: routes, limits, isolation between accounts, hosting records, deletion and export.
- Merge: two devices converge whatever the order, deletions, ties, the same chunk twice, a corrupted chunk, a newer format, clock differences, a store deleted from another device.
- Hooks: a test fails if a write path misses the transaction scope.
- E2E on Chromium and WebKit, axe clean: two browser contexts on one account converge, including a deletion.

## Consequences

- One table on the server and no new service. On the free plan sync cannot cost money, and the limits keep it from filling the database. They do not make a daily D1 outage impossible, only unlikely.
- The server cannot check what it stores: a learner can keep up to 10 MiB of arbitrary bytes that only they can read back. A bad chunk harms only its own account, as each device validates what it receives.
- Between devices with different clocks, "latest" can be the wrong one for decks, words and notes. Reviews are not affected. Accepted and documented.
- Turning sync on changes what survives account deletion, which is the price of the strict reading.
- Every write path to the synced tables must hold `syncEntities` in its transaction: a small change in each, enforced by the hook and a test.
- A learner's first upload can take several requests, and a new device reads one chunk per request. Compaction or a move to R2 is the way out if that becomes slow.
