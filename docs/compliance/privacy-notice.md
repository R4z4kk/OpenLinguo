# Privacy notice

This is the text of the public privacy notice. The page that shows it is published in M7 with the legal notices (ADR 0009), and the sections still missing are written then. Until that page exists, Profile › AI says where the key is kept (#181), and the AI notice says what each AI button sends (#207). The analysis behind this text is in [ai.md](ai.md).

## AI features

**Off until you set them up.** The 5-sentence challenge and the journal work without AI. To get corrections, word suggestions, French translations or stories, choose a provider in Profile › AI and paste your own key. The providers are Groq, OpenRouter, Mistral, or a server running on this device, such as Ollama.

**Straight from your device to your provider.** A request leaves only when you press an AI button. It goes from your browser to the provider you chose, and nothing goes through OpenLinguo: we receive nothing, keep no logs and run no analytics. Your provider handles the request under its own terms and privacy policy, which you accepted when you created your key. Like any website, it also sees your IP address.

**What each button sends.** The same list is shown under each button, as « Sent: … ».

| Button | Sent to your provider, with your key and the chosen model |
|---|---|
| « Test the connection » | A request for the list of models, then a short test question that contains nothing about you |
| « Correct » on a challenge | The word and your 5 sentences |
| « Correct » on a journal entry | The entry |
| « Suggest words » | The themes you ticked, your short interests field and your level |
| « Translate to French » | The Chinese word, its pinyin and its English meanings |
| « Write the story » | Your level, a theme, a length and up to 20 words from your learning steps |

Every request also contains OpenLinguo's instructions to the model and the language of the interface.

**Your key.** By default, the key is stored in your browser (IndexedDB) on this device, so you don't have to paste it each time. With « Don't remember the key », it stays in memory only and is gone when you close the app. Either way, it is never put in your backup file, never synced and never sent anywhere but to your provider. « Forget the key » deletes it. Your provider, your model and whether you have seen the AI notice are stored the same way.

This storage is strictly necessary for the service you ask for, so it needs no consent (Loi Informatique et Libertés, art. 82). We mention it here, as the CNIL recommends (délibération 2020-092, para 49).

Use a key made only for OpenLinguo, with a spending cap if your provider offers one.

**Training on what you send.** Check whether your provider trains its models on your requests, and refuse it if you can:

- Groq is not allowed to train on your requests unless you permit it ([services agreement](https://console.groq.com/docs/legal/services-agreement), §4.2).
- OpenRouter does not store your requests unless you opt in. The companies behind some of its models may keep your requests or train on them: check your OpenRouter privacy settings ([data collection](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/privacy/data-collection)).
- Mistral's free mode may train on what you send and receive, until you opt out ([Mistral help](https://help.mistral.ai/en/articles/347617)).
- With a server on this device, nothing leaves the device.

**Sensitive information.** Don't write about your health, religion, political opinions or sex life in the journal or in your interests: your provider receives whatever you send. The CNIL advises avoiding personal data in such requests as far as possible ([CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/les-questions-reponses-de-la-cnil-sur-lutilisation-dun-systeme-dia-generative)).

**AI answers are labelled.** Every answer shows « Generated by AI · model ». It is stored with a record of the provider, the model and the date, and with nothing about you ([specification](ai.md#4-provenance-the-public-detection-specification)). Your challenges, journal entries, texts, corrections and French translations are in your backup file.

## Looking up names on Wikidata

**Off until you press it.** Reading a text works without it. In the unknown-words panel, « Chercher sur Wikidata » looks up the character sequences that the dictionary does not know, such as names of people, places and organizations, on Wikidata, the free knowledge base of the Wikimedia Foundation.

**Straight from your device to Wikimedia.** A request leaves only when you press that button, after a notice that you see before the first one. It goes from your browser to the Wikidata query service (`query.wikidata.org`), run by the Wikimedia Foundation in the United States, and nothing goes through OpenLinguo: we receive nothing, keep no logs and run no analytics.

**What is sent.** The same list is shown under the button, as « Envoyé : … ».

- The unknown character sequences of your text, 50 per request at most. They are written in the web address of the request, so they appear in Wikimedia's request logs. Neither the sentences, nor the rest of the text, nor its title are sent.
- Your IP address and your browser's user agent, as for any website, and a header that names the app and its repository.
- No cookie and no referrer: the address of the page you are reading is not sent.

**What Wikimedia keeps.** The Foundation records your IP address temporarily. Under its [data retention guidelines](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Data_retention_guidelines), request logs with your IP address and user agent are deleted, aggregated or de-identified after at most 90 days, and its system backups can keep data for up to 5 years. Its [privacy policy](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Privacy_policy) says how to ask it for access to or removal of your personal information. The analysis behind this text is in [wikidata.md](wikidata.md).

**What you get back.** Wikidata's structured data is public domain (CC0). Nothing is applied to your words until you accept it.
