# Data sources and licenses

Checked on primary sources on 2026-10-09 (issue #8). Once a dataset is imported, its exact version, retrieval date, sha256 and maximum age live in `data/manifest.json`; this file records what may be used and under which obligations.

What the datasets lack or had to repair (missing French translations, doubtful or rejected entries, unresolved readings, unsupported characters) is gathered in [`GAPS.md`](GAPS.md), generated by `pnpm data:gaps` and reviewed every month.

Code is AGPL-3.0. Datasets keep their own licenses. Share-alike datasets combined together (CC-CEDICT 4.0 + CFDICT 3.0 + Wiktionary) are distributed under **CC BY-SA 4.0** (BY-SA 3.0 allows adaptations under a later version).

- The CJK fonts are split by `tools/data-pipeline` (subset-font, HarfBuzz) into slices ordered by HSK 2025 band, then GF0025-2021, then the other characters of the shipped datasets by frequency. Dataset characters a font lacks (91 for Noto Sans SC, 40 for WenKai GB, all rare) are listed in each `missing.tsv` and fall back to the next font.
- Fallback (#67, decided 2026-10-12): the 91 characters (50 in extension B, 15 C, 2 D, 8 E, 5 F, 11 G; WenKai's 40 are among them) come from rare CC-CEDICT, CFDICT and French Wiktionary entries and one Make Me a Hanzi hint, none graded. Every font stack ends with OpenLinguo Han Fallback, after Noto Sans SC: Plangothic P1 draws 80 of them, P2 the other 11, and the characters no self-hosted font draws would be listed in `data/font-plangothic-p2/missing.tsv` (none today, tracked by the gap report).
  - Options measured: Plangothic as one slice per source file (chosen): 14 KB + 3 KB, downloaded only by a page that shows one of these characters, then cached; one file per character: 548 to 904 bytes each (median 704, of which about 480 bytes of font overhead), 64 KB for the 91 and 91 more `@font-face` rules; keeping the system font: no download, but empty boxes wherever the system lacks the glyph. An HSK 1 page loads no fallback slice either way.
  - Plangothic was chosen because it extends Source Han Sans, of which Noto Sans SC is the Google release, with mainland China glyph forms, and covers extension B to J. Mincho-style fonts covering the planes (GlyphWiki-based Jigmo or HanaMin, BabelStone Han) clash with both stacks, and TW-Kai follows Taiwan forms. No OFL kaishu font covers these planes, so in display text the 40 characters WenKai lacks are drawn in the sans fallback, as they already were by Noto Sans SC for the 53 others.
  - Plangothic has a Regular weight only: bold text is synthesized by the browser. Its name table states "All rights reserved" next to the OFL 1.1 license fields; the repository `LICENSE-OFL.txt` (pinned in `tools/data-pipeline/licenses/`) and the font's license fields (name ids 13 and 14) grant OFL 1.1, and the copyright notice (name id 0) stays in every subset. The pipeline rewrites name ids 1, 3, 4 and 6, drops the typographic, WWS and trademark names, and refuses to build if a reserved name is left.
- OpenType alternates (vertical, full-width and proportional forms) are dropped: horizontal simplified Chinese only. This divides the Latin and punctuation slices by 3 to 4.
- An HSK 1 page (its 246 characters, punctuation and pinyin) loads 67 KB of Noto Sans SC and 51 KB of WenKai GB; the slices published on npm (frequency order) would load 368 KB and 587 KB.

## Notes

### HSK 2025 syllabus

- The syllabus was published by the Center for Language Education and Cooperation and Chinese Testing International (CTI) in November 2025. CTI announced the worldwide launch of the HSK 3.0 exam on **2026-12-13** (official HSKTestOfficial account, September 2026); trial sittings ran in January and September 2026.
- The official PDF (406 pages, sha256 `ec74ce04…504941`) is encrypted with permissions that allow printing and accessibility extraction only (`/P -1340`, copy and extraction disabled). Extracting it ourselves would bypass a technical protection measure (CPI L.331-5), so it is not parsed (decision 2026-10-09).
- Levels come from the transcription [harukicoder/hsk30](https://github.com/harukicoder/hsk30) (MIT, created 2026-09-01) pinned at commit `36c0d11`, which extracted the PDF and validated its per-level entry counts against the published totals (300 / 500 / 1,000 / 2,000 / 3,600 / 5,400 / 11,000).
- What we verify ourselves: 3,088 recognition characters with the per-level counts 246 / 125 / 284 / 441 / 431 / 413 / 1,148; 10,896 distinct words (the 11,000 entries include homographs); levels 1–7 with the lowest level kept; every graded reading exists in CC-CEDICT. The exact per-level entry totals cannot be recomputed from the collapsed transcription.
- The transcription's pinyin is truncated for some words (下雨 → `xià`); it is only used to choose between CC-CEDICT readings of polyphones, and unresolved words are listed in `data/hsk-2025-words/issues.tsv`.
- Permission to extract and redistribute the word → level and character → level facts was requested from CTI (kaoshi@chinesetest.cn). CTI declined (reply logged 2026-10-14): any use of HSK content needs official authorization, and no such service is offered for now. The reply asks for no removal, so the transcription stays and the official PDF is not used.
- The GF0025-2021 national standard (Ministry of Education PDF, no restriction, scanned pages) is a different list: 41.5% of shared words change level in the 2025 syllabus, so it is not used for exam levels.
- Only the factual word→level and character→level mapping is stored, with attribution. No editorial content, tasks, topics or grammar material is reproduced. The about page states that OpenLinguo is not affiliated with nor endorsed by CTI or CLEC. Takedown on request from the issuing body.

### GF0025-2021 standard

- Shipped next to the 2025 syllabus as a free referential (decision 2026-10-09): the official PDF has no restriction, and as an administrative document of state organs it is likely outside copyright (Copyright Law of the PRC, art. 5; not legal advice). It is also the fallback if CTI asks for the 2025 levels to be removed (#56).
- The OCR is verified exactly against the standard: 500 / 772 / 973 / 1,000 / 1,071 / 1,140 / 5,636 words (11,092 entries, 10,954 headwords once variants and homographs are merged), 3,000 recognition characters (6 × 300 + 1,200), 1,200 handwriting characters (300 / 400 / 500), contiguous numbering. One OCR-misread index (1856 for 1836) is repaired and listed in `data/gf0025-2021-words/repairs.tsv`.
- The list has no pinyin: 404 polyphones get the level on every reading and are listed in `issues.tsv`.
- It is not the exam list: only 58.5% of the words shared with the 2025 syllabus keep the same level.

### CEFR-J

- The terms grant free research and commercial use, and modification, "with a proper acknowledgement of the source". Citation is therefore mandatory and is kept.
- Redistribution inside an open-source app is not addressed. The maintainer accepts this residual risk (decision 2026-10-09). Fallback if challenged: frequency-derived A1–B2 levels + Octanove C1/C2.

### CFDICT

- A 2016 Pleco forum post questions the provenance of some unofficial CFDICT versions; only the official download is used.
- The official XML has 42 corrupted bytes (stray 0xC2 lead bytes, one broken check mark) and 2 empty glosses, and 4 headwords are written with CJK compatibility ideographs (朗朗, 蘭, 老, 嗀: same look, but no search typed with the unified characters finds them); the pipeline repairs exactly these defects (headwords normalized to NFC) and lists them in `data/cfdict/repairs.tsv`, any other invalid byte fails the build. The `.u8` export silently drops the same apostrophes, so it is not used.
- French glosses are attached to CC-CEDICT entries (47,672 keys: exact match, traditional form stored as simplified, neutral-tone difference, or a tone conflict with a single candidate, CC-CEDICT tone kept and listed in `data/cfdict/tone-conflicts.tsv`). A toneless syllable (`a`, erhua `r`) is the neutral tone. The 8,151 words CC-CEDICT lacks become French-only dictionary entries, searchable but not used for segmentation. 40 entries with an invalid pinyin or a space inside the headword are listed in `data/cfdict/rejected.tsv`.

### French Wiktionary

- Second source of hand-written French glosses (#55); no pivot language, no AI. Same join policy as CFDICT: 6,970 CC-CEDICT keys glossed (1,288 of them without a CFDICT gloss), 1,203 French-only entries. Both sources are kept side by side; the app shows the source of each gloss.
- The extract has no date inside, so its version is the start of its sha256; `kaikki.org` only publishes a moving latest URL.
- The first pinyin reading of each entry is used (1,327 entries have several, listed). Sinogram sections are skipped (61% of their glosses are wiki notes such as HSK levels or stroke counts). Entries without pinyin (889), with an invalid pinyin (85) or a title that is not only Chinese characters (44) are left out, and 27 multi-line glosses (wiki examples) are dropped; all are listed in `data/wiktionary-fr-zh/issues.tsv`, never guessed.

### English Wiktionary (extended lexicon)

- Extended lexicon of the unknown-word resolution cascade (#52, step 3 in #34): it explains a sequence the segmenter splits into single characters and never changes segmentation, which keeps using CC-CEDICT headwords only. Only the words whose simplified and traditional forms are both absent from CC-CEDICT are kept, with their part of speech (`name` for the 10,865 proper names) and English glosses; 77,578 entries CC-CEDICT already has are skipped.
- The extract is 155 MB gzipped, 1.2 GB once decompressed: past the maximum string length of V8, so the pipeline reads it one line at a time. Its version is the start of its sha256, as for the French extract.
- The reading is the first Standard Mandarin pinyin of the entry; pinyin tagged with a dialect, a variant (erhua, phonetic sandhi) or Taiwan only is not used. The 30,699 entries without a Standard Mandarin reading are skipped and counted (mostly dialect words: Hokkien, Cantonese, Wu, Hakka, Xiang…), not listed. Each sense keeps its most specific gloss with its labels (such as "(Cantonese)", "(literary)", "(Taiwan)"), so a regional sense is never shown as standard Mandarin.
- Listed in `data/wiktionary-zh/issues.tsv`, never guessed: 3,956 entries with several readings (mostly mainland vs Taiwan tones, first kept), 1,001 with several simplified forms (first kept), 2,568 titles not only in Chinese characters and 81 invalid readings (erhua with an "r" inside the word, such as 玩兒鬧 wánrnào), left out.
- Shipped apart from the first-run data: `public/data/wiktionary-zh/` has its own `index.json`, is not precached by the service worker and is imported into IndexedDB on demand (`importExtendedLexicon`), for the reader to call before its first lookup; the reader is not built yet. Payload: 6.4 MB of JSON in 7 files, 2.3 MB gzipped.

### jieba (proper names)

- Step 4 of the unknown-word resolution cascade (#53, #34): an offline signal that a sequence no lexicon explains is probably a person, place or organization name. jieba gives no meaning and no reading, so the reader shows it as a proposal (« Nom de personne probable », "Probable person name"), never as a fact. Segmentation is unchanged.
- Source: `jieba/dict.txt` at commit `67fa2e36e72f69d9134b8a1037b83fbb070b9775`, the last commit of the project (2020-02-15): 349,046 lines `word frequency tag`, none malformed, one duplicate word (B超, not a name). The license is MIT (`LICENSE`, "Copyright (c) 2013 Sun Junyi"; the README says MIT too): `data/jieba-names/jieba-MIT.txt` is that file, copied to `/data/licenses/jieba/LICENSE.txt` and linked from the About page.
- Provenance: the author built the dictionary from the People's Daily 1998 segmented corpus, the MSR segmentation corpus and novels segmented with ICTCLAS (jieba issue 7, 2012); the Changelog mentions the Sogou 2006 lexicon. Only the word and its tag are kept, no frequency. wordfreq, already shipped, includes the Jieba word list.
- Kept: the six name tags, mapped to a kind: `nr` and `nrfg` → person, `ns` → place, `nt` → organization, `nrt` (transliterated names of people and places alike) and `nz` (other proper names) → name. Words whose form is a simplified or traditional headword of CC-CEDICT, CFDICT, the French Wiktionary or the English Wiktionary extended lexicon are skipped (23,116): the reader explains them first. 89,004 words remain (63,398 nr, 410 nrfg, 9,787 ns, 3,898 nt, 4,348 nrt, 7,163 nz); the 7 words not only in Chinese characters (AT&T, C#, c#, C++, c++, 小S, 大S) are listed in `data/jieba-names/issues.tsv`.
- Precision, measured 2026-10-12 on UD Chinese-GSDSimp (train, dev and test): of the 4,000 multi-character Chinese gold tokens absent from every shipped lexicon, 2,280 are proper nouns (PROPN); jieba has 428 of them, 336 with a name tag. Among the unknown tokens jieba tags as a name, 87% are proper nouns (336 of 385): `nrt` 97% (93/96), `nr` 89% (133/150), `ns` 82% (80/98), `nz` 75% (30/40), `nrfg` 0/1. The frequency band makes no difference, so no frequency filter; the other tags hold only 12% proper nouns. Random samples of the dictionary itself look noisier (idioms, fragments such as 阿紫觉) because real text rarely contains them: hence a proposal, not a fact.
- Shipped apart from the first-run data, like the extended lexicon: `public/data/jieba-names/` has its own `index.json`, is not precached and is imported into IndexedDB on demand (`importNames`). Payload: 2.2 MB of JSON in 9 files, 0.5 MB gzipped.

### Make Me a Hanzi and hanzi-writer-data

- `dictionary.txt` is LGPL-3.0-or-later and derived from Unihan: `data/makemeahanzi/` ships the project's `LGPL` file (Unicode notice + LGPL-3.0) and the GPL-3.0 text the LGPL incorporates, and its README states that the data has been modified (Unicode notice, condition c). All 9,574 characters are kept (decomposition, radical, etymology); `definition`, `pinyin` and `matches` are dropped.
- Stroke data comes from the immutable npm tarball `hanzi-writer-data@2.0.1` (sha256 pinned), read with an in-house tar reader; `ARPHICPL.TXT` is copied from the same tarball. Only the characters of the two shipped referentials are kept: all 3,088 HSK 2025 and 3,000 GF0025-2021 characters (3,143 distinct) have stroke data, missing ones would be listed in `data/hanzi-writer-data/missing.tsv`. Served from the project origin, never from the jsDelivr CDN: imported into IndexedDB with the dictionary (`strokes.json`, 8.3 MB), with `ARPHICPL.TXT` published next to it in `/data/licenses/` and linked from the About page (as are the Make Me a Hanzi LGPL and GPL texts).

### wordfreq

- Only the large Chinese list (`wordfreq/data/large_zh.msgpack.gz`) is used, pinned at commit `912caf6`, the last data release of the frozen project; it is decoded by an in-house MessagePack reader (no new dependency). The Chinese list combines Wikipedia, OpenSubtitles 2018, SUBTLEX-CH, NewsCrawl and GlobalVoices, Google Books Ngrams, OSCAR, Twitter and the Jieba word list; the dataset README credits each of them (SUBTLEX-CH must be credited and stay identified as free data).
- Each bucket becomes a Zipf frequency (log10 of the occurrences per billion words), kept only for the simplified headwords of CC-CEDICT, CFDICT and French Wiktionary: 88,668 of 130,507 headwords. The dictionary search ranks results by exact match, then level, then this frequency (#26).
- The frequency belongs to a written form: every reading of a polyphone shares it, the level tells the readings apart. wordfreq segments Chinese with Jieba, so a word Jieba splits has no frequency (32 of the 10,896 HSK 2025 words, such as 不客气 or 有的); it ranks after the words of its level that have one.
- The wordfreq README asks that conversions keep the attribution and license with the data: the shards carry them in their README, and the dictionary page credits wordfreq next to the results.
